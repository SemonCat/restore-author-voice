# AI-pattern diagnostic

Patterns are clues, not forbidden tokens. Judge function, frequency, genre, and source support.

## Content

Watch for inflated significance, promotional haze, vague attribution, superficial interpretation, forced balance, overgeneralized sourcing, and generic conclusions. Prefer a sourced fact, a named actor, or deletion. Mark missing evidence instead of inventing it.

## Structure

Watch for repeated “not X but Y” pivots, negative runways, forced groups of three, false ranges, rhetorical setup with an immediate answer, punch-line endings on every paragraph, uniform rhythm, summary sandwiches, excessive headings or bold-label lists, and false agency that hides who acted.

Keep a real contrast, real three-part set, or passive voice when it expresses the meaning better.

## Language

Watch for throat-clearing, sycophancy, chatbot sign-offs, inflated substitutes for “is” or “did”, vague jargon, synonym cycling, stacked hedges, fashionable vocabulary without domain meaning, mechanical punctuation, and mannered prose that substitutes flourish or ornamental metaphor for a direct statement. Prefer literal wording when it says the same thing more precisely. Do not run global replacement. A precise or quoted word is not a defect.

## Voice recovery

After cleanup ask whether the prose lost an actual opinion or uncertainty, turned specifics into summaries, became uniformly terse, added unsupported first person or attitude, or could appear unchanged under an unrelated author's name. Plain prose is acceptable; fabricated personality is not.

## Artifacts

Remove unintended collaboration phrases, model limitations, search commentary, unresolved placeholders, broken Markdown, citation handles, tool IDs, AI-added tracking parameters, and editor meta-commentary the reader did not request. For a rewrite, labels such as “Rewritten” or “What changed”, repeated recaps, and generic follow-up offers are artifacts unless the venue or user calls for them.
